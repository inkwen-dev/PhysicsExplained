# What is the third law of thermodynamics?

## Short Answer

The third law of thermodynamics states that as temperature approaches absolute zero, the entropy of a perfect crystal approaches zero. This law explains why absolute zero cannot be reached and establishes absolute zero as the reference point for entropy calculations.

## Detailed Explanation

### Background

The third law of thermodynamics completes the foundation of thermodynamics, providing crucial insights into the behavior of matter at the lowest possible temperatures and establishing fundamental limits on what is physically possible. Understanding this law helps us comprehend why absolute zero is unattainable and how entropy behaves at extreme conditions.

This law is essential because it connects temperature, entropy, and the fundamental limits of cooling. It explains why we can approach absolute zero but never quite reach it, and it provides a reference point for measuring entropy. The third law has profound implications for low-temperature physics, material science, and our understanding of the universe's fundamental limits.

Understanding the third law connects to many fundamental physics principles. The principles behind the third law relate to concepts like [What is entropy?](what-is-entropy), which describes disorder and reaches its minimum at absolute zero, and [What is absolute zero?](what-is-absolute-zero), which is the temperature limit described by the third law.

### Scientific Principles

The third law of thermodynamics works through several key principles:

1. **Entropy at absolute zero**: For a perfect crystal (perfectly ordered structure), entropy approaches zero as temperature approaches absolute zero. This means perfect order is only possible at the lowest temperature.

2. **Unattainability principle**: The third law implies that absolute zero cannot be reached in a finite number of steps. Each cooling step becomes less effective as you approach absolute zero, requiring infinite steps to reach it.

3. **Absolute entropy reference**: The third law establishes absolute zero as the reference point for entropy, allowing calculation of absolute entropy values rather than just entropy changes.

4. **Perfect crystal requirement**: The law applies to perfect crystals. Real materials have imperfections and residual entropy even at very low temperatures, but perfect crystals would have zero entropy at absolute zero.

5. **Residual entropy**: Some materials have residual entropy even at absolute zero due to disorder that cannot be removed (like molecular orientations in ice), showing that perfect order isn't always achievable.

### Real Examples

- Low-temperature research: Scientists studying materials near absolute zero observe how entropy decreases as temperature drops, but perfect zero entropy is never achieved, demonstrating the third law's predictions.

- Superconductivity: Some materials become superconductors at very low temperatures, where entropy approaches zero. The third law helps explain why these low-entropy states are only possible at very low temperatures.

- Quantum mechanics: At temperatures near absolute zero, quantum mechanical effects dominate, and the third law connects classical thermodynamics to quantum mechanics, showing how entropy behaves at quantum scales.

- Material properties: Understanding the third law helps predict how materials behave at low temperatures, including thermal expansion, heat capacity, and other properties that approach zero as temperature approaches absolute zero.

- Cryogenics: Applications in cryogenics (like liquid helium systems) approach but never reach absolute zero, demonstrating the unattainability principle of the third law.

## Practical Applications

### How It Works in Daily Life

Understanding the third law helps us in many ways:

1. **Low-temperature research**: Scientists use the third law to understand and predict behavior of materials at extremely low temperatures, enabling research in superconductivity, quantum mechanics, and fundamental physics.

2. **Cryogenics and cooling**: Engineers designing cooling systems understand the limits imposed by the third law, knowing that absolute zero cannot be reached and designing systems that approach it as closely as possible.

3. **Material science**: Understanding entropy at low temperatures helps develop materials with specific low-temperature properties, such as superconductors and materials for space applications.

4. **Thermodynamic calculations**: The third law provides the reference point for calculating absolute entropy values, essential for thermodynamic analysis and engineering design.

5. **Fundamental physics research**: The third law connects thermodynamics to quantum mechanics and helps scientists understand the fundamental limits of physics and the behavior of matter at extreme conditions.
