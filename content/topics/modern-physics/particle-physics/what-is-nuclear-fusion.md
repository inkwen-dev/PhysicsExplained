# What is nuclear fusion?

## Short Answer

Nuclear fusion is the process where two light atomic nuclei combine to form a heavier nucleus, releasing enormous energy. Fusion powers the Sun and stars, where hydrogen nuclei fuse to form helium. Fusion requires extremely high temperatures and pressures to overcome the repulsive force between positively charged nuclei.

## Detailed Explanation

### Background

Nuclear fusion is the process that powers stars and could potentially provide clean, abundant energy on Earth. Understanding nuclear fusion helps us comprehend how stars shine, how elements are created, and how we might harness fusion for energy. This knowledge is essential for understanding astrophysics, nuclear physics, and potential future energy sources.

Fusion demonstrates how combining light nuclei releases energy through mass-energy conversion (E=mc²), with the product nucleus having slightly less mass than the reactants. This mass difference converts to energy. By exploring nuclear fusion, we can better understand stellar processes and potential energy technologies.

Understanding nuclear fusion connects to many fundamental physics concepts. The principles relate to concepts like [What is nuclear fission?](what-is-nuclear-fission), which is the opposite process, and [What are quarks?](what-are-quarks), which make up the nuclei that fuse.

Nuclear fusion was first understood in the 1920s when scientists realized that stars must be powered by nuclear reactions. Hans Bethe worked out the details of stellar fusion in the 1930s, explaining how stars produce energy. On Earth, controlled fusion has been a goal for decades, with experimental reactors like ITER (International Thermonuclear Experimental Reactor) working toward achieving net energy production from fusion.

### Scientific Principles

Nuclear fusion works through several key principles:

1. **Coulomb barrier**: Positively charged nuclei repel each other due to the Coulomb force. To fuse, nuclei must overcome this repulsion, requiring high kinetic energy (high temperatures, millions of degrees).

2. **Quantum tunneling**: Even at high temperatures, nuclei often don't have enough energy to overcome the barrier classically. Quantum tunneling allows fusion at lower temperatures than classically expected, enabling stellar fusion.

3. **Mass-energy conversion**: Fusion products have slightly less mass than reactants. This mass difference converts to energy via E=mc², releasing enormous energy—fusion releases millions of times more energy per mass than chemical reactions.

4. **Fusion reactions**: Common fusion reactions include: hydrogen fusion (H + H → He), deuterium-tritium fusion (D + T → He + n), and helium fusion (He + He → Be). Each releases energy.

5. **Temperature and pressure**: Fusion requires extreme conditions—temperatures of millions of degrees and high pressures. Stars achieve this through gravity, while fusion reactors use magnetic confinement or inertial confinement.

6. **Fusion fuel**: The most promising fusion reaction for power generation is deuterium-tritium fusion, which requires lower temperatures than other reactions. Deuterium is abundant in seawater, while tritium can be produced from lithium.

7. **Plasma state**: At fusion temperatures, matter exists as plasma—a state where electrons are separated from nuclei. Plasma must be contained using magnetic fields or other methods because it would destroy any material container.

### Real Examples

- The Sun: the Sun fuses hydrogen into helium, releasing energy that powers solar radiation. About 600 million tons of hydrogen fuse per second, converting mass to energy via E=mc².

- Stars: all stars fuse elements, with fusion type depending on star mass and age. Main sequence stars fuse hydrogen, while older stars fuse heavier elements, creating elements up to iron.

- Hydrogen bombs: thermonuclear weapons use fusion, with fission bombs providing the extreme temperatures needed to initiate fusion reactions, releasing enormous explosive energy.

- Fusion reactors: experimental fusion reactors (like tokamaks) attempt to achieve controlled fusion on Earth, using magnetic fields or lasers to create fusion conditions.

- Element creation: fusion in stars creates elements heavier than hydrogen and helium, with stellar fusion producing elements up to iron, and supernovae creating heavier elements.

## Practical Applications

### How It Works in Daily Life

Understanding nuclear fusion helps us in many ways:

1. **Energy production**: Fusion could provide clean, abundant energy, with fusion fuel (deuterium from water) abundant and fusion producing no long-lived radioactive waste.

2. **Astrophysics**: Understanding fusion explains how stars work, how they produce energy, and how elements are created, advancing our understanding of the universe.

3. **Element creation**: Fusion in stars creates elements essential for life, with understanding fusion explaining how elements heavier than hydrogen formed.

4. **Technology development**: Fusion research advances plasma physics, materials science, and engineering, developing technologies with applications beyond energy.

5. **Fundamental understanding**: Understanding fusion helps comprehend nuclear forces, quantum mechanics, and energy conversion, providing insights into fundamental physics.
