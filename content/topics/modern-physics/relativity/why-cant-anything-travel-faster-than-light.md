# Why can't anything travel faster than light?

## Short Answer

Nothing can travel faster than light because reaching light speed would require infinite energy. As objects approach light speed, their mass increases and time dilates, requiring exponentially more energy. At light speed, these effects become infinite, making it physically impossible for anything with mass to reach or exceed this limit.

## Detailed Explanation

### Background

The light speed limit is one of the most fundamental constraints in physics, affecting everything from space travel to particle physics to our understanding of causality. Understanding why nothing can exceed light speed helps us comprehend relativity, why certain things are impossible, and how the universe enforces this limit. This knowledge is essential for understanding modern physics and the nature of reality.

This limit isn't arbitrary or technological—it's built into the structure of spacetime itself. The speed of light represents a fundamental barrier that preserves causality and the structure of physics. By exploring why this limit exists, we can better understand relativity and the universe's fundamental laws.

Understanding why nothing can exceed light speed connects to many fundamental physics concepts. The principles relate to concepts like [What is relativity?](what-is-relativity), which explains the speed limit, and [What is time dilation?](what-is-time-dilation), which is one consequence of approaching light speed.

### Scientific Principles

Nothing can exceed light speed through several key principles:

1. **Infinite energy requirement**: To accelerate an object with mass to light speed requires infinite energy according to E = mc²/√(1-v²/c²). As speed approaches c, the denominator approaches zero, making energy infinite.

2. **Mass increase**: Relativistic mass increases as speed approaches light speed: m = m₀/√(1-v²/c²). At light speed, mass would be infinite, requiring infinite force to accelerate further.

3. **Time dilation**: As speed increases, time dilates more dramatically. At light speed, time would stop, making further acceleration impossible from the object's perspective.

4. **Causality preservation**: If anything could travel faster than light, effects could precede causes, violating causality and fundamental physics principles that maintain the order of events.

5. **Universal constant**: The speed of light is a fundamental constant of nature, the same for all observers. This constancy, combined with relativity, creates the speed limit.

### Real Examples

- Particle accelerators: particle accelerators accelerate particles to 99.99%+ of light speed but can never reach exactly c, demonstrating how energy requirements increase exponentially as speed approaches the limit.

- Space travel: spacecraft can approach but never reach light speed. Even with advanced propulsion, the energy needed becomes infinite as speed approaches c, making light-speed travel impossible.

- Cosmic speed limit: the expansion of the universe and all physical processes respect the light speed limit, demonstrating its universal nature and fundamental importance.

- Information transmission: no information can travel faster than light, limiting communication speeds and ensuring causality is preserved throughout the universe.

- Thought experiments: relativity thought experiments show that faster-than-light travel would allow time travel and violate causality, demonstrating why the limit exists.
